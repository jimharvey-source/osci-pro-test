About the Pro tool

A development tool for people whose work depends on whether what they say actually lands, and who want something sharper than the free version can give them.

What the Pro is

The Pro instrument is a sixty-question assessment that measures ten subscales of charisma, grouped under two dimensions: Confidence and Social Skills. It returns four readings rather than two: your score on each dimension, an Authenticity Index that measures consistency across audiences, and a Personal Impact score that measures whether what you say lands. It places you in a quadrant, gives you a banded interpretation of where each subscale sits, and identifies the two subscales most worth your attention now.

It is designed for people who already know roughly where they want to develop, and who want something more precise than a personality test. The free Open-Source Charisma Indicator places you on the map. The Pro version tells you which of ten specific skills will most repay your attention, and whether your delivery is doing the work your substance deserves.

What the Pro is not

It is not a personality test. It does not produce a Big Five profile, a Myers-Briggs type, or any other trait-level description. People with very different personalities can produce identical Pro scores. The instrument is silent on what is going on underneath.

It is not a clinical instrument. Low scores indicate development opportunities. High scores indicate consolidated capability. Nothing here is designed for use in psychological or psychiatric diagnosis.

It is not a recruitment or selection tool. It is a self-report instrument designed for voluntary personal development. Used as a gatekeeping tool, it would produce both unfair outcomes and predictable legal exposure for anyone using it that way.

It is not a measure of moral character. The Authenticity Index measures whether your social behaviour is reliably available across audiences, which is a different question from whether you are a sincere or honest person. The instrument cannot see motive. It can see consistency.

The Personal Impact score is not a test of public speaking. It samples how well your message lands across ordinary working situations, not the moment of platform delivery. A strong speaker with thin everyday communication will score lower than they expect. The room is the truer measure than the stage.

How the Pro differs from the free version

The free Open-Source Charisma Indicator uses forty questions across eight subscales to place you in your charisma quadrant. It is designed as the entry point to the model: simple, fast, free.

The Pro version adds two subscales to make ten, and produces two further readings the free tool cannot see:

Ten subscales, not eight

Each dimension is broken into five distinct competencies rather than four. The two new subscales pick up the gap between intent and impact, and the gap between how you behave with people who matter to you and everyone else.

The Authenticity Index

A separate reading that picks up whether your warmth, attention and courtesy are reliably available across audiences, or noticeably uneven. Two people with identical dimension scores can have meaningfully different Authenticity Index bands.

Personal Impact

A separate reading of whether what you say actually lands. It has two components: Message Craft (how the message is built) and Voice and Presence (how it is delivered). The two together answer one question: does the thing this person says land.

What you actually receive

The Pro generates a personalised PDF report of around twenty-three pages. It includes your four readings and your quadrant placement, a banded interpretation of each of the ten subscales, a personal narrative reading of where you are right now, an Authenticity Index page, a Personal Impact page that reads your impact score against the social skills underneath it, two priority subscales fully expanded with specific practices, summaries of the remaining eight subscales, and references to the methods at the back of the book.

The Personal Impact page is the one most readers find most useful. It tells you whether your delivery is doing the work your substance deserves, or running ahead of it. Strong delivery on top of genuine social skill is what makes a good communicator compelling. Strong delivery on top of thin social skill is the polished speaker the room does not trust. The report names the gap when it sees it.
